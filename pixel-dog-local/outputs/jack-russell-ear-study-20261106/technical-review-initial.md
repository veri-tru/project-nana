## Findings

**JET01 — MAJOR, unresolved.** `comparison.png`, row 2 TOP, lower ear (approximately x1395–1478, y588–645): the leaf remains a broad, low, fully folded patch like row 1. The upper ear in this same view has a raised tan base with a short folded cap. Both ears in row 2 FRONT and BACK have raised bases. This is an unexplained left/right and cross-view structural difference. Both prompts require paired semi-erect ears, and the README explicitly says asymmetry was not introduced. Fix the lower TOP ear to show a raised base and short forward-turning tip consistent with its mate, preserving the face, then directly review all 15 cells in the new file. Exact mirror symmetry is unnecessary; equivalent fold configuration is necessary.

**JET02 — MINOR, unresolved.** `comparison.png`, non-ear facial shading across the board, especially row 1 FRONT/BACK: approved A's stepped shadow transitions are softer and somewhat lighter in the new image. The second edit also changes non-ear pixels despite its preserve-all-pixels wording. Head silhouette, muzzle proportions, dot eyes, white coat, tan ears and short neck remain recognizably the selected character, so the observed local shading drift is recorded as minor rather than a different character. Do not claim pixel invariance. Recheck meaningful non-ear drift after any correction; increased smoothing or face-shape drift would require renewed severity assessment. A future strict ear-only deliverable should preserve the approved non-ear region in the actual final asset.

For evidence of drift rather than a quality threshold, exact aligned RGB patches yield final-versus-approved mean absolute channel differences: row 1 side cheek (120,280)-(245,330), 1.92/255; front face (740,280)-(860,330), 7.52/255; back middle (1020,270)-(1140,330), 6.10/255. Against first ear iteration those patches are 1.62, 3.84 and 2.37 respectively. These patch measurements do not prove invariance elsewhere and do not substitute for visual judgment.
